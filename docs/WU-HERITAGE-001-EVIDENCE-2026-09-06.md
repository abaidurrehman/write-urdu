## 4. Lifetime scale exercise

The first public heritage release deliberately avoids assuming historical traffic was higher before generative AI. The founder believes the site was more popular in earlier years, but comparable historical Product Pulse telemetry is not available yet.

Use a simple constant-current-rate scale exercise as a transparent baseline:

- 0.60–0.92 million characters/week
- × 52 weeks/year
- × 10 years
- ≈ **312–478 million characters**

To translate that into a human-readable word equivalent, use an explicitly rough editorial assumption of **about 6 characters per word including spacing**. This is not a linguistic measurement of every Urdu writing style; it is a scale conversion only.

- 312m / 6 ≈ **52 million word-equivalents**
- 478m / 6 ≈ **80 million word-equivalents**

Therefore the first release may say:

> **`Tens of millions of Urdu words — estimated, not counted.`**

It should not publish a more precise lifetime number as fact.

## 5. Book/page equivalents

For an illustrative comparison only:

- at 80,000 words per full-length book, 52–80 million words ≈ **650–1,000 books' worth of text**;
- at 500 words per page, 52–80 million words ≈ **104,000–160,000 pages**.

Safe public wording:

- `Enough text to fill hundreds of full-length books.`
- `The upper end of a simple current-rate extrapolation approaches a thousand 80,000-word books.`
- `More than a hundred thousand 500-word pages of text at the same illustrative scale.`

Avoid:

- `Users wrote 1,000 books.`
- `Thousands of books were written on Write Urdu.`
- `Hundreds of thousands of school hours.`

Those would convert an illustrative equivalent into a false observed use case.

## 6. Human-purpose wording

The site can truthfully describe the **kinds of writing it supports**, for example:

- school and university assignments;
- applications and formal letters;
- poetry, ghazals, nazms, essays and stories;
- family and personal messages;
- notices and business writing;
- social-media posts, captions and statuses;
- invitations and announcements.

Until real user stories arrive, frame these as possibilities/use cases, not measured historical categories.

Preferred heading:

> `What might those millions of words have been?`

Preferred supporting line:

> `We do not inspect the writing itself, so these are the kinds of everyday Urdu tasks Write Urdu supports — not a tracked breakdown of people's private text.`
