# WU-SHAADI-001 — Pakistan Wedding Invitation Evidence Ledger

**Research date:** 2026-09-18  
**Purpose:** Preserve the evidence that shaped the epic so later implementation does not silently turn a Pakistani invitation system into a generic wedding-site clone.

This ledger distinguishes source-backed observations from product hypotheses. It is not a claim that any one vendor or academic sample represents all Pakistani weddings.

---

## 1. Academic evidence — physical Pakistani wedding invitation structure

**Source:** Nazish Amjad & Fakhira Riaz, “Genre Analysis: A Study of Pakistani Wedding Invitation Cards”, Global Political Review, 2020.  
**URL:** https://www.gprjournal.com/article/genre-analysis-a-study-of-pakistani-wedding-invitation-cards  
**DOI:** 10.31703/gpr.2020(V-I).18  
**Sample:** 50 Baraat invitation cards, 50 Mehndi invitation cards and 50 wedding-card envelopes, collected primarily from Rawalpindi/Islamabad with some from other cities.

### Supported observations

- The study identifies eleven component moves in Baraat cards, ten in Mehndi cards and five on wedding-card envelopes.
- Baraat-card moves include an opening, inviter/host names, request for guest presence, couple identification, day/date, time, venue and family-member/RSVP-style information.
- Openings can include Tasmiya/Bismillah, Quranic material/translation or poetic wording.
- Baraat inviters were commonly parents/guardians in the sample, with other family members appearing depending on circumstances.
- The groom's name was obligatory in the study's Baraat sample; bride naming varied, including daughter-of formulations.
- Full venue address appears as part of the venue move.
- “RSVP”, “Looking forward” and equivalent family-member sections appear.
- Mehndi wording was generally less formal than Baraat wording in the study.
- Mehndi ceremony name, day/date, time and venue were obligatory moves in the sample.
- The outer envelope's invitee name was the only obligatory envelope move.
- Invitee names could include “with family” depending on how many members were invited.

### Product consequence

The digital invitation should preserve the social function of the envelope by making the guest household a first-class record, not just a text placeholder.

### Limitations

- The research was published in 2020.
- The physical-card sample is geographically concentrated.
- It describes observed conventions, not mandatory etiquette for every family.
- The product must therefore support variants rather than enforcing one social norm.

## 2. Academic evidence — Pakistani online wedding cards

**Source:** “Discourse Analysis of Pakistani Wedding Invitation Cards during Pandemic (COVID-19)”, Journal of the Research Society of Pakistan, University of the Punjab, Vol. 59 No. 1, 2022.  
**URL:** https://pu.edu.pk/images/journal/history/PDF-FILES/16_59_1_22.pdf  
**Sample described in abstract:** 120 online wedding cards collected from Facebook between 27 March and 13 June 2020.

### Supported observations

- The paper explicitly studies online Pakistani wedding invitations.
- It reports that social, cultural and religious norms remain visible in online invitation cards.
- Its move analysis includes opening, parents' names, wedding programme, looking-forward elements and guest names.

### Product consequence

Moving from paper to digital does not imply stripping away Pakistani invitation structure. The digital product should retain recognizable cultural framing while improving logistics and personalization.

### Limitations

- Pandemic-era behavior is atypical.
- Facebook cards from a short 2020 period are not a national adoption survey.
- Do not infer current market share of digital invitations from this study.

## 3. Current Pakistani printer workflow — Pearl Envelopes

**Source:** Pearl Envelopes current wedding-card product pages.  
**Representative URL:** https://pearlenvelopes.pk/products/royal-script-wedding-card

### Current detail-collection pattern observed

The vendor asks customers to provide:

- bride and groom name;
- groom S/o parent name;
- bride D/o parent name;
- date;
- reception/gathering timings;
- dinner timings;
- venue;
- name(s) for “Awaiting to Welcome”.

Product pages also advertise WhatsApp proofing before print.

### Product consequence

The structured model must support:

- parentage/lineage;
- more than one labelled time per event;
- “Awaiting to Welcome” family names;
- proof/review before final output.

A simplistic Bride / Groom / Date / Venue form would lose real information already used by Pakistani printers.

### Limitation

One commercial vendor cannot define nationwide convention. Use these fields as supported options, not compulsory fields.

## 4. Current Pakistani printer workflow — Bloom & Beyond

**Source:** Bloom & Beyond, Nikkah invitation-card process article.  
**URL:** https://bloomnbeyond.pk/blog/nikkah-invitation-cards-lahore-2026

### Observed workflow

The vendor describes:

1. discussing theme, palette and wording over WhatsApp;
2. creating a digital proof;
3. customer approval/revision;
4. printing/hand finishing.

It also describes Urdu calligraphy, Bismillah/ayat selection, custom layouts and monograms.

### Product consequence

Write Urdu can remove waiting/revision friction by converting the printer's “send details → receive proof → correct proof” cycle into live structured editing while still preserving a proof-before-final principle.

## 5. Current Pakistani print/digital hybrid — Aprints

**Source:** Aprints, “Wedding Card Printing in Pakistan — Complete Guide 2026”.  
**URL:** https://aprints.pk/wedding-card-printing-in-pakistan-complete-guide-2026-types-designs-prices/

### Supported current examples

The guide lists:

- traditional flat cards;
- scroll invitations;
- box invitations;
- digital wedding cards shared through WhatsApp/social media;
- matching Mehndi/Baraat/Walima sets;
- bride/groom names;
- hosting families;
- event name;
- date/day;
- time;
- venue/full address;
- Google Maps QR;
- optional dress code;
- RSVP contact.

The vendor describes a workflow of selecting a design, sharing text/details, receiving a personalized proof, approving it, and then printing.

### Product consequence

The long-term product should be hybrid:

create once
→ digital card
→ print/PDF
→ envelope/recipient output
→ QR/private interactive invite

It should not assume digital replaces physical cards.

### Limitation

Vendor trend statements such as “most popular” are marketing claims and should not be treated as population statistics without independent data.

## 7. Current interactive Pakistani wedding invitation product

**Source:** GiftPage Pakistan, Royal Invitation Suite.  
**URL:** https://giftpage.pk/templates/wedding-invite

### Observed features

The current product advertises:

- family-side/custom wording;
- multiple wedding-event inserts;
- multiple labelled timings;
- Pakistani invitation wording;
- venue/address/maps;
- calendar actions;
- dua insert;
- RSVP/Looking Forward groups;
- family contacts;
- private link;
- QR code;
- optional password;
- view analytics.

It also explicitly avoids a default Western attendance-response form in the displayed feature set.

### Product consequence

Interactive wedding links are already understandable in the Pakistani market. Write Urdu's opportunity is not simply to invent a wedding website; it is to make the setup dramatically simpler and combine it with Urdu/Roman-Urdu, household personalization and card/print output.

## 10. Product hypotheses derived from the evidence

The following are hypotheses, not established facts.

### H1 — “Digital envelope” is the strongest differentiation

Because physical invitation envelopes traditionally identify the invitee and may specify “with family”, guest-household personalization can feel culturally familiar rather than novel.

Test:

- do creators use individual/couple/family scope?;
- do they assign different events to different households?;
- does personalization increase completion/share rates?

### H2 — One wedding data model is more valuable than one card

Current workflows repeatedly ask customers to send details to a designer/printer for a proof.

Hypothesis:

A structured live composer that regenerates outputs after one date/venue/name change will remove meaningful friction.

Test:

- project completion;
- edits after first preview;
- regeneration frequency;
- multiple output types per project.

### H3 — Hybrid print + WhatsApp is safer than “digital replaces print”

Current commercial offerings support both physical and digital formats.

Hypothesis:

Offering PNG/PDF/print/QR from one project will address more families than a digital-only invitation website.

Test:

- output mix;
- number of users using both print/PDF and digital share;
- QR usage after print slice.

### H4 — Roman/Latin guest-name → Urdu review is a Write Urdu moat

Name conversion is adjacent to Write Urdu's established capabilities.

Hypothesis:

Users with Latin-script guest lists will value editable Urdu suggestions enough to use bulk personalization.

Test:

- percentage of guest names entering suggestion flow;
- edit/confirmation rate;
- generation completion after conversion.

### H5 — RSVP should be optional and culturally minimal

The evidence establishes RSVP/family-contact conventions but does not prove broad adoption of Western-style wedding-site questionnaires.

Hypothesis:

A simple attend/decline + bounded party count is more acceptable than a large RSVP form.

Test after link usage exists.

## 11. Explicit claims we must NOT make

Do not claim without new evidence:

- a specific percentage of Pakistani weddings use digital invitations;
- WhatsApp has replaced printed cards;
- all Pakistani weddings contain the same events;
- all Baraats/Walimas follow one host model in every family/community;
- one vendor's field list is the national standard;
- digital invitations are cheaper for every household;
- RSVP is already a common Pakistani digital-wedding behavior;
- video invitations are “the future”;
- QR codes are used by a measured majority.

## 12. Research gaps to revisit before Slice 4+

Before public personalized links/RSVP become a major implementation investment, run another evidence round on:

- actual Write Urdu user interest in wedding-related card content;
- Search Console demand for Urdu wedding/Shadi/Nikah card queries;
- Pakistan mobile/WhatsApp sharing friction;
- privacy expectations for public vs private invitation links;
- older-relative accessibility;
- whether households prefer one master link or one link per guest;
- whether RSVP party count feels useful or socially awkward;
- printer requirements for PDF size/bleed/QR;
- Urdu/English/bilingual preference;
- common mistakes in date/time/venue wording;
- acceptable retention/deletion period for hosted invitations.

## 13. Evidence confidence summary

**High confidence for product architecture:**

- Pakistani invitation cards contain recurring structural information.
- guest names/“with family” are part of physical invitation-envelope practice.
- multi-event/timing/venue/family fields are currently used by Pakistani print vendors.
- digital/WhatsApp and interactive invitation products exist.
- online invitations preserve cultural/religious structure.

**Medium confidence:**

- hybrid print + digital will be attractive to a meaningful Write Urdu segment.
- Roman-name → Urdu assistance can differentiate the product.
- household/event-scoped personalization will be valued.

**Low/unknown until product evidence:**

- market size;
- repeat usage;
- willingness to use RSVP;
- willingness to store invitations online;
- demand for video;
- preferred monetization model.

Implementation should preserve the high-confidence architecture while progressively validating the medium/low-confidence behavior.
