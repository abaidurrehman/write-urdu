What transliteration does

It converts the way a word sounds in Roman letters into a likely Urdu-script spelling. It does not translate the sentence into another language.

When you type mera naam Ali hai, the editor can suggest میرا نام علی ہے. Suggestions are committed word by word when you press Space.

You can select an alternative suggestion, edit the Urdu directly, or use the on-screen Urdu keyboard when you need a specific character. The final wording remains your responsibility.

Transliteration is not translation

Translation changes meaning from one language to another. Transliteration keeps the language and changes how its sounds are represented in writing.

Why one Roman spelling can have several answers

Roman Urdu has no single official spelling system. Context, regional pronunciation and the surrounding words can change which Urdu spelling is most useful.

1

Review the sentence

Read the complete Urdu sentence rather than accepting an isolated word. Grammar and meaning often make the right suggestion clearer.

2

Expect alternatives

A writer may type khair, kher or khayr for related sounds. Try another spelling when the first result looks wrong.

3

Check names and specialist terms

Names, places and uncommon words may need direct keyboard input or an alternative suggestion instead of the first result.

A practical proofreading routine

Important writing should be reviewed before it is copied, shared or exported.

  1. Type one short phrase and press Space to see the suggestion.
  2. Read the Urdu result from right to left and check names, numbers and punctuation.
  3. Use the suggestion list or Urdu keyboard to correct an ambiguous word.
  4. Use Clean spacing and find-and-replace tools when a longer draft is ready.
  5. Copy or export only after the final Urdu text has been reviewed.
