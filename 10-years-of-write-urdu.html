These are the kinds of writing Write Urdu supports, not a tracked breakdown of people's private text. The writing itself is not inspected to decide whether it was homework, poetry, a letter or a social post.

THE PART WE CANNOT COUNT

We can estimate how much was written. We cannot tell you what was written.

Write Urdu did not keep a decade of people's homework, letters, poems or private messages just so we could produce a bigger statistic today. Our recent product measurements use aggregate counters rather than the text itself. Much of the writing created here left through copy, download, print or sharing and continued its life somewhere else. That is exactly how it should be.

TRANSPARENT METHODOLOGY

How we estimated the lifetime scale

The headline is deliberately less precise than the calculation. The calculation is useful for scale; the rounded language is more honest about what we do and do not know.

Show the assumptions and calculation

In one recent week, aggregate writing-length buckets contained 1,067 non-zero sessions. Using the midpoint of each bounded bucket, then treating the open-ended 2,500+ bucket as a bounded 2,500–5,000-character scenario, those sessions represent roughly 0.6–0.9 million characters.

  • That is a derived range from writing-length buckets, not an exact character counter.
  • Extending the same current scale across 52 weeks and 10 years gives roughly 312–478 million character-equivalents.
  • Using a rough editorial conversion of about six characters per word including spacing gives about 52–80 million word-equivalents.
  • At 80,000 words per full-length book, that is enough text to fill hundreds of books; the upper end approaches a thousand books' worth.
  • Historical traffic certainly varied. We do not make it larger just because earlier years may have been busier.

That is why the public headline says “tens of millions” rather than pretending we have an exact lifetime word counter.

THE DECADE

From a simple Urdu-writing need to a broader writing platform

We keep the timeline broad where exact historical release dates have not been reconstructed. Product history should become more specific only when the evidence does.

July 2016

Write Urdu begins online

A simple problem shaped the product: make it easier to get thoughts into readable Urdu on devices where English-letter input was often the easiest starting point.

Early years

Writing first

English-letter Urdu and direct Urdu input stayed at the centre — a browser tab people could use for a sentence, a message or something much longer.

Over time

From text to finished work

Richer formatting, exports and creative tools expanded the journey from getting Urdu onto the screen to preparing documents and shareable designs.

2026

More ways into Urdu

Voice input, Urdu-language surfaces, writing templates, modern document flows and moderated community publishing broadened the ways people can write, keep and share Urdu.

Next decade

Keep Urdu easy to write

The technology will change again. The product goal remains simpler: reduce the friction between having something to say in Urdu and being able to write it well.
