Urdu fonts: Nastaliq versus Naskh

Nastaliq gives Urdu its familiar flowing, diagonal rhythm. Naskh uses a more regular baseline and is often easier to fit into small interfaces, tables and mixed Urdu-English layouts. The best choice depends on what you are making and how small the text will appear.

The practical difference between Nastaliq and Naskh

Both can render Urdu correctly. Their spacing, rhythm and visual density are different, so the surrounding layout matters as much as the font name.

Nastaliq: traditional Urdu character

Nastaliq is strongly associated with Urdu books, newspapers, poetry and calligraphic presentation. Its diagonal movement and stacked forms need generous line height, especially in longer passages.

Naskh: compact and predictable

Naskh follows a more regular baseline. It is a useful starting point for instructions, forms, tables, compact layouts and mixed Urdu-English text where predictable wrapping matters.

Which Urdu font style should you choose?

Start with the job the reader needs to complete, then preview the final size rather than judging a large sample in isolation.

1

Poetry, quotes and greetings

Try Nastaliq first when the traditional Urdu look is part of the design. Give lines enough room so marks and stacked forms do not collide.

2

Documents and interfaces

Try Naskh when the text must stay readable at smaller sizes, align with controls or tables, or sit beside Latin text.

3

Social images

Either can work. Preview the finished card at phone size and choose the version that keeps the message legible rather than the one that only looks best while zoomed in.

There is no universal “best Urdu font”

A beautiful Nastaliq face can become cramped in a small control, while a compact Naskh face may feel too formal for a poetry card. Treat font choice as part of the layout, not as a ranking of scripts.

Quick Urdu readability checks

These checks catch more real problems than choosing a font from its name alone.

  1. Give Nastaliq enough line height for stacked and descending forms.
  2. Check Urdu and Latin words together when the design uses both scripts.
  3. Preview social graphics at the size they will actually be viewed on a phone.
  4. Use sufficient text/background contrast and avoid decorative backgrounds behind dense writing.
  5. Check the final exported image or document instead of relying only on the editor preview.
